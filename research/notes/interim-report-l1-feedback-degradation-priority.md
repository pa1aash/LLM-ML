# Interim report: L1

**Locus question:** What is the priority of the claim "iterative feedback degrades LLM-guided NAS" against RZ-NAS (ICML 2025) and the 2025-26 agentic/multi-agent NAS work?
**Flavor:** dialectical

## What the corpus already said

The loci brief itself (`research/loci.json`, L1 `why_load_bearing`) pre-loaded a specific factual claim: that GENIUS "already reports non-monotonic iteration behaviour" and that "RZ-NAS and CoLLM-NAS report reflective/iterative LLM-NAS working," concluding "the unqualified claim cannot survive both." The brief flagged GENIUS Section 6 as "the strongest candidate for a partial scoop." This investigation verifies that framing against full text and finds it needs a correction: **CoLLM-NAS, not GENIUS, is the strongest scoop candidate**, because CoLLM-NAS runs an actual ablation showing an iterative-feedback mechanism causing measured degradation, whereas GENIUS's note is a single hedged sentence. No prior corpus note had close-read GENIUS's Appendix A.3 numeric tables or CoLLM-NAS's memory-retention ablation in detail; that reading is the primary contribution of this note.

## What the new sources say

No new URLs were fetched. All nine `must_read_note_ids` are full text already in the vault (word counts 8k-201k), and the four specific factual questions in the brief were fully settled by close reading of that full text, consistent with the constraint to "prefer settling from the vault." Zero of the 2 permitted supplementary fetches were needed. What follows is the close-reading record, source by source.

**GENIUS / "Can GPT-4 Perform Neural Architecture Search?"** ([[can-gpt-4-perform-neural-architecture-search]], full text, 9,853 words). Section "6 Limitations," subsection "Limited control and inscrutability," verbatim:

> "On the NAS-Bench-201 benchmark (see more details in Appendix A.3.), we find that later iterations under-perform earlier iterations in some cases, and it is unclear why this should be the case given that: (i) our prompt requests improved performance, (ii) our experimental evidence suggests that GPT-4 is capable of providing improved performance. We believe future work on this problem is particularly valuable."

This is exactly one sentence, embedded among two other unresolved caveats in the same subsection (API-black-box non-reproducibility, benchmark contamination) — it is not isolated as more significant than those. There is **no ablation**. The paper never removes feedback and compares to zero-shot as a controlled variable; "in some cases" is not quantified anywhere in the main text. I checked Appendix A.3's raw per-iteration numeric tables (Table 7, NAS-Bench-Macro, Temperature=1) to see how far the anomaly actually goes. Trial 1: accuracy by iteration T=0..9 is 90.90, 92.40, 92.30, 92.53, 92.63, 92.66, **92.97 (peak, T=6)**, 92.56, 92.50, 92.56 — i.e., iterations 7-9 sit below the T=6 peak. Trial 2 peaks at T=3 (92.85) then dips before partially recovering to 92.78 at T=9. Trial 3 peaks at T=2 (92.82), dips to 92.34 at T=4, recovers only to 92.66 at T=9. **In every trial shown, the local peak occurs mid-run and the run ends below that peak — but in every trial, T=0 (the zero-shot proposal) is the single worst value of the entire trajectory, by 1.0-1.9 accuracy points.** The paper's own numbers therefore show feedback-driven iteration strictly beating zero-shot on average, with non-monotonic *local* noise along the way. This is a different, much weaker claim than "feedback degrades the process," and does not isolate feedback as the causal mechanism for the dip — the same subsection blames unresolved API non-determinism ("even with the temperature set to 0, we observe some variation in GPT-4 responses") as a co-occurring confound.

**RZ-NAS** ([[icml-poster-rz-nas-enhancing-llm-guided-neural-architecture-search-via-reflectiv]], full text, 11,395 words, ICML 2025). Mechanism: a single-lineage mutate-evaluate-reflect loop, where "the reflective module guides the LLM to reflect on mutation performance and generates targeted suggestions for further iteration improvements" — but the "performance" signal reflected on is a **Zero-Cost proxy score**, not trained accuracy ("we utilize Zero-Cost proxies instead of training architectures to reduce computational resources and time cost"). Ablation, verbatim (Appendix A.4.1):

> "The 'w/o Reflection Module' variant is created to reflect on previous architecture performance and error exceptions. The absence of reflection leads to a performance drop and increased exceptions... This suggests that the absence of reflection leads to a lack of iterative improvement, as the system fails to learn from past successes and failures... Without this module, the RZ-NAS framework essentially operates in a static, trial-and-error manner, resulting in lower search efficiency and less competitive performance compared to the full RZ-NAS setup."

This directly contradicts the unqualified thesis, with an ablation, at ICML 2025. All experiments use GPT-4o as the default LLM (generality checks add LLaMA 3.1 and Claude 3.5) — no small or quantized model is tested anywhere in the paper.

**EvoPrompting** ([[evoprompting-language-models-for-code-level-neural-architecture-search]], full text, 17,981 words). Scaffold: crossover/mutation operator πθ is "a 62B parameter PALM model... additionally fine-tuned on a corpus of 64B tokens" of Python; population-based evolutionary loop, 10 rounds, 10 prompts/round, 16 samples/prompt, soft prompt-tuning between rounds on MNIST-1D and CLRS. The "EvoPrompting (random parents)" ablation removes fitness-based selection (Step 10, `GetTop`) and replaces it with random parent selection — the direct analog of "remove feedback-driven selection." Verbatim:

> "The random parents baseline plateaus the quickest, reaching a maximum fitness by the time approximately 200 individuals have been generated. Furthermore, the maximum fitness it reaches is significantly worse than that of the other experiments... prompting with randomly selected parents combined with prompt-tuning is no more effective than naive prompting alone."

Feedback-driven selection is load-bearing; removing it collapses performance to the naive zero-shot-equivalent baseline. This is the cleanest ablation in the corpus for "feedback helps," but the scaffold (62B fine-tuned model, external persistent population archive across generations, prompt-tuning weight updates between rounds) is far from a single small quantized model reasoning in one context.

**LLMatic** ([[230601102v8-llmatic-neural-architecture-search-via-large-language-models-and-qua]], full text, 8,053 words). Verbatim abstract claim: "While LLMs struggle to conduct NAS directly through prompts, LLMatic uses a procedural approach, leveraging QD for prompts and network architecture to create diverse and high-performing networks." This sentence is a **design motivation**, not an ablated finding — LLMatic never runs LLM-with-feedback vs. LLM-without-feedback within its own method. Its empirical support is indirect: LLMatic's own Table 1 shows GENIUS (the feedback-driven direct-prompting baseline) essentially tied with or slightly *below* random search — 93.79% vs. 93.70% (CIFAR-10), 70.91% vs. 71.04% (CIFAR-100, GENIUS *below* random search), 44.96% vs. 44.57% (ImageNet16-120) — while LLMatic (QD-wrapped) reaches 94.26/71.62/45.87, near the 94.47/74.17/47.33 optimum. This bears on "is naive LLM-direct-prompting NAS competitive with non-LLM baselines" (weak circumstantial support, mixed), not on "does adding feedback beat zero-shot" — LLMatic never isolates that variable.

**GPT-NAS** ([[230505351v4-gpt-nas-evolutionary-neural-architecture-search-with-the-generative]], full text, 11,348 words). Mechanism check: GPT-NAS fine-tunes a GPT model once on architecture data and uses it as a frozen block-reconstruction prior inside a standard genetic algorithm (crossover/mutation/selection with a linearly-decaying elimination rate). There is **no natural-language performance-feedback prompt loop** — the "GPT model" never receives an in-context textual accuracy report and is never asked to reason about why a design under- or out-performed a prior one. Its two ablations (elimination-rate sweep, acceleration-strategy sweep, Section V-B) test GA hyperparameters, not feedback. GPT-NAS is mechanistically off-target for this locus.

**CoLLM-NAS** ([[250926037v2-collm-nas-collaborative-large-language-models-for-efficient-knowledg]], full text, 11,313 words, arXiv Sept 2025 v2). Two-role architecture: stateful Navigator LLM (persistent memory, refines search strategy each round from accumulated results) + stateless Generator LLM ("focusing exclusively on the current strategy without retaining any memory"). The ablation that matters here is the memory-retention sweep (Section 4.4, Fig. 6), verbatim:

> "for low-complexity datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100), optimal performance is achieved without memory retention in either LLM, indicating that iterative feedback alone suffices for simpler tasks. Conversely, for high-complexity datasets (e.g., ImageNet-16-120, ImageNet), preserving the Navigator LLM's memory while disabling the Generator's yields optimal results, confirming the necessity of historical trajectory in demanding scenarios. Notably, we observe that retaining the Generator LLM's memory induces progressive noise accumulation, leading to performance degradation."

This is a controlled ablation (not an anecdote), with quantified deltas (roughly 0.1-0.6 accuracy points depending on dataset, largest on ImageNet-16-120 and ImageNet), that isolates one specific variable — whether the *generation* role accumulates raw iteration history in its own context — and finds that accumulating it **degrades** performance via "noise accumulation." Their production system is deliberately engineered to avoid this: the deployed Generator is stateless by design, precisely because unrestricted feedback accumulation was found to hurt. A second, related but distinct ablation (SiLLM-NAS: merging Navigator+Generator into one LLM handling both roles) shows the merged single-LLM design underperforms the two-role design, but the paper attributes part of that gap to worse initial-population quality, not solely to feedback accumulation — so I keep these two findings separate and do not conflate them.

**Agent-Oriented Planning in Multi-Agent Systems** ([[241002189-agent-oriented-planning-in-multi-agent-systems]], full text, 12,254 words). This is not a NAS paper — it is general multi-agent LLM task-decomposition/planning. Its ablation table (Table 2) shows removing the plan detector drops accuracy 43.7% → 36.6%, and removing the reward model drops it 43.7% → 38.7%: feedback/evaluation loops help in this adjacent domain. Relevant only as background context that "feedback helps" is the default finding outside NAS too, not as direct NAS evidence.

**Evolutionary Computation in the Era of LLM survey** ([[240110034v3-evolutionary-computation-in-the-era-of-large-language-model-survey-a]], full text, 20,661 words, last revised **29 May 2024**). This survey catalogs GPT-NAS, LLMatic, EvoPrompting, and additional 2024-era work (Guided Evolution / "Evolution of Thought" by Morris et al., which "allows the LLM to propose improved modifications for new individuals based on the performance of past mutations," GPTN-SS, Jawahar et al.'s LLM-as-performance-predictor). None of these are reported by the survey as showing feedback degrading performance. Critically, the survey **predates RZ-NAS, CoLLM-NAS, and the entire 2025-26 agentic-NAS wave** by 8-16 months, so it cannot be read as evidence about that later landscape — it only establishes that, as of mid-2024, GENIUS's single-sentence anomaly was uncorroborated anywhere else in the reviewed literature.

**NAS: Insights from 1000 Papers** ([[neural-architecture-search-insights-from-1000-papers]], full text, 31,239 words). Checked for LLM-guided-NAS or feedback-effect content; found none material to this locus (it is a general NAS methodology/estimand survey, more relevant to the deferred L3 locus). Not further used here.

## Evidence synthesis

Two things are true simultaneously in this corpus, and the tension between them is the crux of the locus.

### Position A: Iterative feedback improves LLM-guided NAS — the field's dominant published result

RZ-NAS (ICML 2025) and EvoPrompting both report controlled ablations in which removing the feedback/selection component causes a measured performance collapse. RZ-NAS: removing the reflection module causes "a performance drop and increased exceptions," reducing the system to "static, trial-and-error." EvoPrompting: removing fitness-based parent selection makes the search "no more effective than naive prompting alone" and causes the search to "plateau" at "significantly worse" fitness. GENIUS's own headline numbers point the same way in aggregate — every reported trial shows the *zero-shot* first proposal (T=0) as the single worst point in the entire trajectory, with feedback-driven iteration reaching top 0.1-0.93 percentile architectures. Agent-Oriented Planning shows the same direction in an adjacent (non-NAS) multi-agent LLM setting. This is the numerically stronger, ablation-backed, more-cited camp, and it directly outnumbers the counter-evidence in ablation rigor.

### Position B: Iterative feedback can degrade LLM-guided NAS — a narrower, mechanism-specific but real published result

CoLLM-NAS (Sept 2025) runs a controlled ablation that isolates exactly one variable — whether a generation-role LLM accumulates its own raw iteration history in-context — and finds that it "induces progressive noise accumulation, leading to performance degradation," severely enough that the authors' production system deliberately makes that role stateless. This is not an anecdote; it has a named mechanism (noise accumulation from unfiltered history), a controlled variable, and quantified deltas across four datasets, with the effect size scaling with task complexity (negligible on CIFAR-10/100, clearest on ImageNet-16-120 and ImageNet). GENIUS's Section 6 sentence is real but far weaker: unquantified, un-ablated, hedged as "unclear why," and — on the paper's own numbers — describes *local* non-monotonicity within a trajectory that still strictly beats zero-shot in aggregate, which is a substantially narrower claim than "feedback degrades LLM-guided NAS."

The two positions are not actually in contradiction once the mechanism is separated from the surface claim. RZ-NAS's feedback is a *single, current-iteration* zero-cost-proxy score reflected on immediately, in a design that never lets raw history accumulate unfiltered — mechanically the same discipline CoLLM-NAS converges on for its Generator role. EvoPrompting's feedback is *external, population-level* fitness-based selection across generations (an archive, not accumulating conversational context). Both of the "helps" results come from designs that curate what feedback the model sees. CoLLM-NAS's "hurts" result comes specifically from *not* curating it — letting an LLM's own context balloon with unfiltered past iterations. GENIUS and the subject paper's described design (single model, feedback appended within one growing context, per the L4 locus's characterization of the subject repo) sit in the "uncurated accumulation" bucket that CoLLM-NAS shows degrades, not the "curated, single-signal reflection" bucket that RZ-NAS and EvoPrompting show helps.

## Committed position

The unqualified thesis — "iterative performance feedback degrades rather than improves LLM-guided neural architecture search," stated as a general, mechanism-agnostic claim — is dead on priority grounds: it is directly contradicted by two controlled ablations (RZ-NAS, ICML 2025; EvoPrompting) that show feedback/selection strictly helping, and it is not actually supported by its own strongest cited ally — GENIUS's own per-trial numbers show feedback beating zero-shot in every reported trajectory, with the "under-perform" observation describing local mid-run noise, not a net feedback-vs-zero-shot deficit. But a narrower, mechanism-specific formulation survives and is now **partially scooped, not open**: "when an LLM's own uncurated iteration history is allowed to accumulate inside a single growing context (rather than being filtered into a synthesized strategy or an external archive), iterative feedback degrades output quality, especially as task complexity rises" is already published and ablation-verified in CoLLM-NAS (Sept 2025) via its Generator-memory experiment, which the subject paper must cite and distinguish itself from. The load-bearing reason for this position is that CoLLM-NAS's ablation isolates the identical structural variable (single-context accumulation of raw feedback vs. curated/reset state) that the subject paper's design instantiates, whereas GENIUS's anecdote does not isolate anything and RZ-NAS/EvoPrompting's ablations run in the opposite direction under a different, curated-feedback mechanism.

- **Position:** The general claim is refuted by RZ-NAS and EvoPrompting's own feedback-ablations and is not actually supported by GENIUS's per-trial data; the surviving, defensible formulation is that *uncurated single-context accumulation of iterative feedback* degrades LLM-NAS proposal quality on harder tasks, a claim already ablation-verified by CoLLM-NAS (Sept 2025) — so the subject paper is partially scooped on mechanism, and its remaining novelty must rest on a different axis (small/quantized single-model regime, or the "collapse to a single template" observation, neither of which any prior-work source in this corpus reports).
- **Confidence:** high on "the unqualified general claim is dead" (3 independent full-text sources — RZ-NAS ablation, EvoPrompting ablation, GENIUS's own per-trial numbers — converge); medium on "CoLLM-NAS specifically is the load-bearing prior work to distinguish from" (based on one paper's one ablation, albeit a clean, quantified, mechanism-isolating one; I have not verified whether CoLLM-NAS's noise-accumulation account has itself been challenged or replicated elsewhere, since no other 2025-26 paper in this corpus tests the same variable).
- **Boundary conditions:** This position holds for LLM-guided NAS specifically, where "feedback" means a training/evaluation signal (trained accuracy or a zero-cost proxy) reported back into a prompt for the same or a paired LLM to act on. It does not extend without further evidence to non-NAS program/design search (Agent-Oriented Planning's non-NAS multi-agent result was not stress-tested for the accumulation failure mode) or to feedback mechanisms that are structurally curated (external archives, distilled strategies) rather than raw-context accumulation — those remain in Position A's territory. It also does not extend to a claim about *zero-shot single-call collapse to a template*, which no source in this batch addresses at all (that is a distinct empirical claim requiring L2's diversity-collapse literature, not L1's).
- **What would change this position:** (a) A demonstration that CoLLM-NAS's noise-accumulation finding fails to replicate, is an artifact of their specific prompt design, or was later retracted/corrected — the CoLLM-NAS note here is a single arXiv v2 preprint, not yet a peer-reviewed venue publication, so its ablation, while methodologically clean, has not passed independent review; (b) evidence that GENIUS's Appendix A.3 anomaly, when re-examined across the full appendix (I sampled Table 7's three trials; I did not exhaustively check every table in every appendix subsection A.3 across all benchmarks) rather than the NAS-Bench-Macro table alone, shows genuine net feedback-vs-zero-shot degradation rather than local non-monotonicity — that would restore GENIUS as a stronger, independent second scoop; (c) a 2025-26 agentic-NAS paper not in this corpus that ablates single-context feedback accumulation and finds it *helps*, which would directly contest CoLLM-NAS's finding and reopen Position A as dominant even at the mechanism-specific level.
- **Evidence weight:** 2 controlled ablations support "feedback helps" in curated-signal designs (RZ-NAS, EvoPrompting); 1 controlled ablation supports "feedback (specifically uncurated context accumulation) hurts" (CoLLM-NAS); 1 un-ablated anecdotal observation is genuine but weaker than initially framed and, read closely, does not even net-contradict the "helps" camp (GENIUS); 1 source states struggle-with-direct-prompting as motivation without ablating feedback specifically (LLMatic); 1 source is mechanistically off-target (GPT-NAS, no textual feedback loop); 1 source is non-NAS corroboration for "helps" (Agent-Oriented Planning); 1 survey is too early (May 2024) to bear on the 2025-26 question at all (EC survey).

## Open questions

- Has CoLLM-NAS's noise-accumulation ablation been cited, challenged, or replicated by any other 2025-26 paper? This corpus does not contain a second source testing the same variable — worth a targeted search if budget allows in a later phase.
- I sampled only one appendix table (NAS-Bench-Macro, Temperature=1) from GENIUS's Appendix A.3; the paper reports several more benchmark/temperature combinations there. A full pass might turn up a trial where the *final* iteration is net worse than T=0, which would meaningfully strengthen GENIUS as an independent scoop rather than the weaker reading reached here.
- Whether RZ-NAS's zero-cost-proxy feedback signal is itself valid across benchmarks (the NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero size-confound question) is explicitly out of scope for this locus and assigned to the L5 investigator; if that proxy is shown to be unreliable, RZ-NAS's ablation still shows "reflection helps versus no reflection" but says less about whether the *signal being reflected on* is trustworthy — worth reconciling with L5's finding at the comparisons stage.
- No source in this corpus tests a small (<7B) or quantized LLM in any of these mechanisms; every ablation here uses a frontier-scale or fine-tuned research-scale model (GPT-4o, 62B PaLM, or a purpose-fine-tuned GPT). This is the cleanest gap the subject paper could claim, and it is untouched by any must-read source in this locus.
