## What the new sources say

I did not need to fetch new sources for RZ-NAS or NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero — both were already vault-resident at full text and answered (a) and most of (b)/(c) directly. I did fetch two additional sources (within the 2-source cap) to resolve the one genuine gap: NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero (2022) cannot have evaluated ZiCo, because ZiCo was published in 2023 — after RZ-NAS's own headline ImageNet result is built specifically on ZiCo, this gap was load-bearing enough to chase.

**[[icml-poster-rz-nas-enhancing-llm-guided-neural-architecture-search-via-reflectiv]]** (RZ-NAS full text). This is the primary source for (a). RZ-NAS's own related-work section explicitly cites NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero and acknowledges the exact concern this locus investigates, then does nothing about it in the method: "Recently, benchmarks such as NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero (Krishnakumar et al., 2022), have raised concerns about using them as full replacements for accuracy. These insights motivate the careful selection and combination of proxies in practice." No combination is ever implemented — Algorithm 1's input is "the selected Zero-Cost proxy zc" (singular), and the user prompt "must specify the Zero-Cost method to be applied" (singular). The paper reports five separate proxy variants run one at a time — GraSP, Gradnorm, Synflow, Zen-Score/Zen-NAS, ZiCo (Tables 2–4) — plus a sixth, MAE-DET, for the COCO object-detection experiments (Section 4.3, not evaluated by any source I read). This is the primary evidence for (a): a *menu* of single proxies, not an ensemble.

**[[221003230-nas-bench-suite-zero-accelerating-research-on-zero-cost-proxies]]** (NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero full text). Its own answer to "RQ1: How well do ZC proxies generalize across different benchmarks?" states: "Several methods, such as snip and grasp, perform well on the NAS-Bench-201 tasks, but on average are outperformed by params and flops on the other benchmarks... on the widely used NAS-Bench-201 benchmarks, almost all of them [ZC proxies] perform well." This is the load-bearing sentence for (b): NAS-Bench-201 is the *easy* benchmark for proxy validity — nearly every proxy looks fine there — which matters because NAS-Bench-201 is exactly (and only) where RZ-NAS runs its own correlation check. Its bias table (their Table 3) reports Pearson correlation between proxy value and architecture-size metrics; the relevant rows for RZ-NAS's proxy menu: synflow correlates 0.57 (NB101-CF10) / 0.62 (NB301-CF10) with num. parameters and 0.57–0.58 with cell size on NAS-Bench-201; zen-score correlates 0.68 (NB101-CF10) / 0.99 (NB301-CF10) with num. parameters; grad-norm correlates 0.30/0.51 with num. parameters; grasp correlates -0.03/0.24 with num. parameters and -0.01/0.01 with cell size.

**`zico-zero-shot-nas-via-inverse-coefficient-of-variation-on-gradients-full-text`** (ZiCo, Li et al., ICLR 2023 Spotlight — fetched fresh this session via arXiv PDF, full text, 15,659 words). This is the primary source closing the ZiCo gap for (c). Its own abstract states the state of the field bluntly: "none of the zero-shot proxies proposed to date can actually work consistently better than a naive proxy, namely, the number of network parameters (#Params)." Table 1 (NATS-Bench-TSS = NAS-Bench-201 topology space) reports GraSP KT=0.37/0.36/0.40, Synflow KT=0.54/0.57/0.56, Zen-score KT=0.29/0.28/0.29, #Params KT=0.57/0.55/0.52, ZiCo KT=0.61/0.61/0.60 across CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100/ImageNet16-120 — numbers that match RZ-NAS's own Table 3 baseline rows exactly, confirming RZ-NAS reused this paper's published correlation figures rather than recomputing them. The critical result for the size-confound question is Table 3 (their numbering) on **NATS-Bench-SSS**, the benchmark that varies *only* channel width (32,768 architectures differing solely in per-layer width) — the cleanest possible isolation of "size" as a variable. There, ZiCo's margin over raw #Params nearly vanishes: KT 0.54 vs 0.53 (CIFAR-10, +0.01), 0.55 vs 0.54 (CIFAR-100, +0.01), 0.70 vs 0.65 (ImageNet16-120, +0.05) — compared to margins of +0.04, +0.06, +0.08 on the topology-varying TSS benchmark. Synflow (KT 0.61/0.60/0.39) and Zen-score (KT 0.50/0.52/0.69) actually *beat* ZiCo on SSS for two of three datasets, while GraSP collapses toward zero or negative (-0.09/0.01/0.29) — i.e., on the benchmark that isolates size, the size-confounded proxies get *better*, not worse, which is the confound made visible in the cleanest possible experimental design.

**`zico-bc-a-bias-corrected-zero-shot-nas-for-vision-tasks-full-text`** (ZiCo-BC, Bhardwaj et al., ICCV Workshop 2023 — fetched fresh, full text). This paper's stated motivation explicitly names the general phenomenon NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero identified and extends it to ZiCo specifically: "training-free metrics can be biased towards specific characteristics in neural architectures [citing NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero]... existing zero-shot NAS proxies can exhibit bias towards various factors, such as cell sizes, skip connections, convolutions, number of parameters, etc." Its own empirical finding: "we observe that ZiCo is biased towards thinner and deeper networks, leading to sub-optimal architectures," with the mechanism given precisely: "the metric sums over the number of layers in the network. Consequently, the score grows linearly in number of layers, whereas the gradient statistics grow logarithmically. For networks with repeating blocks, this can lead to deeper models achieving higher ZiCo scores even if they have significantly lower width." The quantified cost: ZiCo-BC "consistently achieve[s] 11–30% lower latency and 0.2–1.1% higher accuracy compared to the models found via the original ZiCo," with a concrete case (ImageNet classification) where uncorrected ZiCo loses 0.9 accuracy points relative to a same-latency baseline (76.8% vs. 77.7%) purely from the depth/width bias.

## Evidence synthesis

RZ-NAS is architecturally a *proxy-agnostic search wrapper*, not a method tied to one metric. Its LLM-reflection module never sees ground-truth accuracy; it sees a single scalar zero-cost score whose identity (GraSP / Gradnorm / Synflow / Zen-Score / ZiCo / MAE-DET) is fixed by the experimenter before the run starts and is passed into the prompt as a `"type"` field. This matters for the framing question because it means the size-confound critique is not a single yes/no check — it has to be evaluated against each proxy RZ-NAS actually reports results with, and the answer is genuinely different proxy by proxy.

For two of RZ-NAS's five proxies — Synflow and Zen-Score — NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero's own bias table shows a strong, directly-on-point size confound (0.57–0.62 and 0.68–0.99 correlation with parameter count, respectively, with Zen-Score's 0.99 on NB301-CF10 being the single worst size confound of any proxy in that table among RZ-NAS's menu). Concretely inside RZ-NAS's own paper, the single largest reported improvement anywhere in the results tables is the Synflow variant on the DARTS/CIFAR-10 space (Table 4): test error drops from 5.9% to 4.3%, a ~27% relative cut — nearly triple the improvement margin of any other proxy variant in that table (GraSP: 6.0→5.4; Gradnorm: 5.7→5.2; Zen-NAS: 2.55→2.50; ZiCo: 2.45→2.41) — and that table, unlike the MobileNet-space tables, states no FLOPs budget constraint, meaning nothing in the reported experiment rules out that the LLM mutation trajectory drifted toward higher-parameter cell configurations that Synflow (and, transitively, test accuracy on CIFAR-10, which is itself weakly size-correlated on this benchmark) rewards independent of genuine architectural quality.

For GraSP, the confound genuinely does not hold — NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero's own numbers put it near zero on every size-bias metric, and ZiCo's independently-reported NATS-Bench-SSS numbers confirm GraSP's correlation with accuracy collapses toward zero or negative on the size-isolated benchmark rather than strengthening, which is the opposite of what a size-riding proxy would do. GraSP's problem instead is that it is simply a weak proxy (KT 0.36–0.40 on NAS-Bench-201, below even the naive #Params baseline's 0.52–0.57) — a different and less damaging failure mode for RZ-NAS's argument than the one this locus was assigned to check.

For ZiCo, the proxy underpinning RZ-NAS's single most citable claim (79.0% Top-1 ImageNet at 450M FLOPs, "over 62 times faster" than DONNA), NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero's finding cannot be checked directly — ZiCo postdates that benchmark by roughly a year. But this is not the same as "undetermined." Two independent, dedicated follow-up papers close the gap and land on the same conclusion NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero reached for the older proxies: ZiCo's own paper shows its edge over the raw parameter-count baseline is real but shrinks to a 0.01–0.05 Kendall's-tau margin on the one benchmark that isolates architecture size as the sole variable (versus 0.04–0.08 elsewhere) — i.e., roughly half of ZiCo's already-modest advantage over "just count the parameters" survives contact with a size-only search space. ZiCo-BC then identifies and quantifies a distinct but structurally analogous confound (a depth/width bias baked into how the metric sums gradient statistics across layers), costing 0.2–1.1 accuracy points and 11–30% latency in matched comparisons, and explicitly frames its own contribution as extending NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero's finding to a proxy that benchmark could not have covered. RZ-NAS's own reported ZiCo baseline (22.0% error = 78.0% accuracy at 450M FLOPs) matches ZiCo's self-reported number (78.1%) almost exactly, and RZ-NAS's "remarkable" 79.0% is a ~1-point absolute gain over that — real, but modest relative to the "SOTA" framing, and not disentangled from the depth/width bias ZiCo-BC documents in the identical FLOPs-budget regime RZ-NAS operates in.

(b) is best answered as: yes, narrowly, and the narrowness is itself informative. RZ-NAS's only rank-correlation validation (Table 3: Kendall's tau, Spearman's rho vs. true test accuracy) is computed exclusively on NAS-Bench-201, across its three built-in datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet16-120) — confirmed by [[200100326v2-nas-bench-201-extending-the-scope-of-reproducible-neural-architectur]] to be three downstream *tasks inside one fixed search space*, not three different benchmarks in the NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero sense (NAS-Bench-101, NAS-Bench-301/DARTS, TransNAS-Bench-101). For the DARTS space, MobileNet/ImageNet space, and COCO detection space — where RZ-NAS's strongest "SOTA" claims live — the paper reports only final search-result accuracy/error after the fact, never a proxy-vs-ground-truth correlation check. NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero's own RQ1 answer states that NAS-Bench-201 is precisely the benchmark where "almost all of them perform well" — RZ-NAS validated its proxies on the one benchmark where validation is least diagnostic, and never validated on the benchmarks where its headline claims actually rest.

### Load-bearing counter-consideration

RZ-NAS's MobileNet-space experiments (Table 5, the source of the ImageNet headline number) are explicitly FLOPs-budget-constrained (450M/600M/1000M), which rules out the crudest version of "the reflection module just learns to propose bigger models." This is a genuine mitigating factor for the ZiCo/Zen-Score flagship claim, though it does not fully neutralize the confound: ZiCo-BC's own comparisons are latency-and-FLOPs-matched and still find a 0.2–1.1-point accuracy cost from ZiCo's depth/width bias, meaning a fixed compute budget constrains *total* size but not the *shape* bias (thin-deep vs. wide-shallow) that both ZiCo-BC and the DARTS-space Synflow result (no budget constraint at all) are consistent with.

## Committed position

The NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero size-confound wedge holds against RZ-NAS, and holds more broadly than the literal citation the query names, because RZ-NAS's own experimental design reuses exactly the proxies NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero flagged (Synflow, Zen-Score) for its results tables, validates rank correlation only on the one benchmark (NAS-Bench-201) that benchmark itself calls the easy case, never validates correlation at all on the search spaces its headline claims are drawn from, and rests its single most citable number on ZiCo — a proxy NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero could not have tested but which two independent, dedicated 2023 papers show carries an analogous, quantified confound (a shrinking edge over raw parameter count on the size-isolated NATS-Bench-SSS benchmark, and a separate, mechanistically-explained depth/width bias worth 0.2–1.1 accuracy points). RZ-NAS's own text cites NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero's warning verbatim in its related work and then declines to act on it — no ensembling, no bias correction, no cross-search-space correlation check — which converts the citation from a mitigating "we were aware of this" into an aggravating "we named the risk and did not test for it." The load-bearing reason this matters for the priority question: a downstream paper reporting that feedback *degrades* LLM-guided NAS is not defeated by RZ-NAS's positive result if RZ-NAS's positive result is itself substantially attributable to a known, named, uncorrected size/shape confound in the proxy it optimizes against — the two papers may not be measuring the same underlying phenomenon at all, which narrows rather than eliminates RZ-NAS's claim to priority.

- **Position:** RZ-NAS's reflective mechanism refines against a single, experimenter-selected zero-cost proxy per run (from a five-proxy menu: GraSP, Gradnorm, Synflow, Zen-Score, ZiCo, plus MAE-DET for detection) rather than an ensemble; its only correlation-based validation is confined to NAS-Bench-201 (the benchmark NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero itself identifies as the case where nearly all proxies already look good); and the NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero size-confound finding demonstrably applies to two of RZ-NAS's five proxies directly (Synflow, Zen-Score) and, via independent 2023 evidence outside NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero's own panel, to a third — ZiCo, the proxy behind RZ-NAS's flagship claim.
- **Confidence:** high for (a) and (b) — these are direct textual facts, cross-checked against exact table structure and confirmed via two independent full-text sources (RZ-NAS itself and ZiCo's own paper, whose numbers match RZ-NAS's Table 3 verbatim). Medium-high for (c)'s overall verdict ("wedge holds and is broader than named") — the size-correlation numbers are exact and multiply-sourced, but the causal claim that RZ-NAS's *reported gains* are substantially confound-driven (rather than substantially genuine, with the confound as a co-occurring but non-explanatory fact) is inference, not something any source directly measures for RZ-NAS's specific runs, because no ablation isolating confound-contribution from quality-contribution exists in this literature for RZ-NAS itself.
- **Boundary conditions:** This applies cleanly to RZ-NAS's DARTS-space Synflow result (Table 4, no stated FLOPs budget, largest reported margin, proxy with a confirmed 0.57–0.62 param-count correlation) and to the general framing of RZ-NAS's validation methodology (narrow, self-serving choice of benchmark). It applies with a genuine mitigating factor — a fixed FLOPs budget — to the MobileNet/ImageNet flagship claim, which constrains total size but not the depth/width shape bias ZiCo-BC documents in matched-budget comparisons. It does not apply to GraSP, which NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero's own numbers and ZiCo's independent SSS reproduction both show is weak but not size-riding.
- **What would change this position:** A published ablation that reports RZ-NAS's search-space parameter-count distribution before vs. after reflection (or an accuracy-vs-FLOPs-matched comparison, as ZiCo-BC ran for vanilla ZiCo) would directly settle whether RZ-NAS's improvement survives controlling for size/shape. If such an ablation showed RZ-NAS's gains persist at matched parameter count and depth/width ratio, this position would not hold — the confound would be present in the proxy but not load-bearing for RZ-NAS's actual reported numbers. I did not find such an ablation in RZ-NAS's own paper (including its appendices on ablations, which cover in-context examples, reflection-module removal, prompt rephrasing, and selection strategy, but never a size-matched control) or in either ZiCo paper for RZ-NAS specifically.
- **Evidence weight:** 2 direct full-text primary sources (RZ-NAS, NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero) with exact matching correlation tables; 2 independent full-text follow-up sources (ZiCo, ZiCo-BC) closing the ZiCo-specific gap with quantified, mechanistically-explained bias figures; 0 sources contradicting the size-confound finding for Synflow/Zen-Score; 1 partial mitigating fact (FLOPs-budget constraint on the MobileNet-space experiments) that narrows but does not eliminate the concern; 0 sources providing the specific size-matched ablation that would fully resolve the causal question.

## Open questions

- Does the size/shape confound survive within RZ-NAS's FLOPs-budget-constrained MobileNet-space search specifically, or is it neutralized by the budget cap? No source runs this exact test on RZ-NAS's own outputs.
- MAE-DET, the zero-cost proxy RZ-NAS uses for COCO object detection (Section 4.3), is untested by NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero, ZiCo, or ZiCo-BC — its size/quality confound status is genuinely unknown from this evidence base.
- Zen-NAS's own paper (fetched as a bonus primary source by the fetcher subagent this session, `zen-nas-a-zero-shot-nas-for-high-performance-deep-image-recognition-full-text`) reports Kendall's tau of 0.91/0.88 for Zen-Score on its own validation setup — dramatically higher than the 0.28–0.29 both RZ-NAS and ZiCo's paper independently report for Zen-Score on NAS-Bench-201. This is a striking self-report-vs-independent-reproduction gap that a later investigator or the corpus critic should verify is an apples-to-apples comparison (different architecture pool / validation protocol could partly explain it) before citing it as evidence that proxy authors systematically overstate their own proxy's quality.
- I did not check whether RZ-NAS's DARTS-space Table 4 experiments (the ones with no stated FLOPs budget) are drawn from a search space where parameter count is free to vary substantially, versus one where the DARTS cell structure keeps parameter counts within a narrow band regardless of operator choice — this would materially affect how much "room" the Synflow variant had to exploit a size confound in practice.
